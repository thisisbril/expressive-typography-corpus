operation_description: >
  The stem of the lowercase i has been removed entirely. The dot remains, floating at its correct height between the h and the d with no vertical stroke connecting it to the baseline. The letterform is inferrable from the floating dot and word context alone, but its body is absent. The i is present only as a hovering remnant of itself.
concept_encoded: Hiding. The body gone, only a trace remaining
semantic_mechanism: >
  The stem is the i's structural body and the stroke that plants it on the baseline to make it physically present in the word. The dot is merely its crown. Suppressing the stem removes the body while leaving the trace. This produces a letterform that hovers and enacts hiding with anatomical precision. The i has withdrawn its body and left only a mark behind. No other letterform in *hide* contains this structural duality of a detachable body and a detachable crown. The partial suppression eventually brings awareness to the concept of hiding, even though the legibility its delayed.
legibility_integrity: Partial
surprise_legibility_ratio: 3
curatorial_note: >
  A self-referential Suppression, which makes its legibility prolonged to notice. The i does not lose its most visible feature, which is the dot. Upon critical observation, the dot remains, floating, as evidence that something was there. What is gone is the body. The hovering dot is more unsettling than a clean absence would be. It marks the place where the i stood before it hid itself. That quality of presence confirmed by a remnant is what exactly contribute to the Suppression. The surprise and legibililty tension of the Suppression would have yielded a 5 if the space occupied by the removed stem of the i is not compressed. That will bring more meaning to the absence of the removed stem.
generative_principle: >
  When a word names an act of concealment or withholding, examine whether any letterform contains a detachable identifying feature whose removal enacts that concept on the letterform itself. The dot of the lowercase i is the strongest candidate in the Latin alphabet. It is structurally essential to the letterform's identity,visually prominent, and physically separate from the stem, making its absence immediately felt. Suppression works most powerfully when the removed element is both maximally present in its normal state and maximally meaningful in its absence.
