primitive_relationship:

    relationship: amplifies
    explanation: > 
      The 2x2 grid arrangement (Inflection) positions the four letterforms to fill four cells. One per cell. This reinforces the concept of FOUR as a countable quantity. The chamfered, stencil-broken geometry (Mutation) is applied across all four letterforms simultaneously, producing a unified visual treatment. However the Mutation delivers no conceptual return while extracting a significant legibility cost that leaves the Inflection as the sole operation earning its semantic payoff.
target_glyphs:
  - F, O, U, R (Mutation)
  - F, O, U, R as a compound unit (Inflection)
operation_description: >
  All four letterforms have been structurally modified through chamfered corners and stencil-style stroke breaks. Curved and sharp terminals replaced by angled cuts, strokes interrupted at intervals. The four letterforms are arranged in a 2x2 grid, with F and O occupying the top row and U and R occupying the bottom row. The whole word is rendered in pale grey. The O's chamfering produces a tail-like artifact that causes it to read as a Q. The F's fragmentation pushes it toward illegibility.
concept_encoded: >
  Intended: FOUR as a quantity, four units filling four cells. Achieved: a partially legible arrangement whose Mutation contributes visual complexity without semantic payoff.
semantic_mechanism: >
  The 2x2 grid is the design's one geometrically precise operation. This is a legitimate and discovered semantic connection. The word's own letter count matches the grid's cell count, and the arrangement makes that equivalence visible. The Mutation does not support this reading. Chamfered, stencil-broken letterforms carry no semantic relationship to the concept of four. Instead they introduce visual complexity that extracts legibility without encoding meaning. The pale grey also compounds the friction.
legibility_integrity: Partial
surprise_legibility_ratio: 2
curatorial_note: >
  A design with one precise semantic operation buried under two layers of counterproductive friction. The 2x2 grid is correct. Four letters, four cells, one per cell, the word enacting its own quantity. Everything else works against it. The chamfered stencil treatment adds no meaning while costing legibility. A strong version of this design would set four clean, legible letterforms in a 2x2 grid and stop there. The grid does all the semantic work. The Mutation is noise.
generative_principle: >
  When a word names a quantity and its letter count matches that quantity, a grid arrangement that gives each letter its own cell is a precise and sufficient semantic operation. Through this operation, the word enacts its own meaning through spatial organization alone. Additional structural modifications to the letterforms are justified only when they contribute to the concept.
