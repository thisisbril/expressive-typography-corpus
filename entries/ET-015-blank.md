operation_description: >
  The l has been rotated 90 degrees from its conventional vertical orientation to horizontal, so that it lies flat at baseline level. The letterform's geometry is entirely intact, but its orientation places it flush with the baseline, where it reads as an underscore or fill-in-the-blank line.
concept_encoded: A blank. An empty space where something should be but cannot be read
semantic_mechanism: >
  The l is the simplest letterform in the Latin alphabet. That geometric simplicity is what makes Inflection so effective here. Rotating the l costs nothing structurally, but it costs the letterform its entire legibility as a letter. A vertical stroke is a letter. The same stroke horizontal is a line, a blank, a gap. The word performs its own meaning by laying its most structurally minimal letterform flat. The l does not disappear, but now becomes the symbol for its own absence. The hairline weight of the typeface reinforces this. At this stroke weight, the horizontal l is indistinguishable from a ruled blank line.
legibility_integrity: Partial
surprise_legibility_ratio: 3
curatorial_note: >
  A precise Inflection whose legibility cost is real. The l rotated to horizontal produces the correct symbol for blankness, but it does so at the expense of immediate readability. The viewer decodes b_ank before blank, meaning surprise and legibility arrive sequentially rather than simultaneously. The concept depends on this gap. The blank must actually feel blank before the word resolves. The hairline weight is doing essential work. At any heavier stroke weight the horizontal l would read as a dash rather than a blank line, and the operation would lose its typographic precision entirely.
generative_principle: >
  When a word names a condition of absence or illegibility, examine whether any letterform is geometrically simple enough that a change of orientation alone suspends its legibility while producing a meaningful symbol. The l is the strongest candidate in the Latin alphabet for this operation. Its single-stroke geometry means that rotation produces no ambiguous residue.
